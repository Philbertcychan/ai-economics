# CoreWeave S-1: reading notes

Working notes for `companies/coreweave.py`. Every figure below comes from the filing itself and
carries a page reference so it can be checked; nothing is taken from a secondary source. Page
numbers are the printed page numbers of the S-1 unless marked `424B4 p.`.

How these tables were filled (2026-09-19): the filing was split into page-numbered text, figures
were extracted section by section, and each one was machine-checked against a verbatim snippet on
the cited page before it was allowed in. That check proves a number is on the page; it does not
prove the label next to it is the best reading, so confirm the ones you rely on while reading.
Prose is condensed; the exact wording is on the cited page.

## Read this first

Traps found while extracting. Each was checked against the page text.

- **Share and per-share figures in the S-1 are pre-split.** A 20-for-1 stock split became effective
  March 14, 2025, after the S-1 was filed; the final prospectus restates every share count, exercise
  price and per-share figure (424B4 p.22). Dollar totals (revenue, net loss, balance sheet, debt)
  are unaffected. Take share data from the final prospectus.
- **The OpenAI agreement is not in the S-1 as first filed.** It appears in the final prospectus
  (424B4 p.33, p.158, p.F-59); see "What the final prospectus changed".
- **Three different depreciation and amortisation figures for 2024.** Total D&A 863,413 thousand in
  the cash flow statement and the Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation (p.F-10); $861 million on property
  and equipment (p.F-29); $843 million within technology and infrastructure expense (p.106). Do not mix them.
- **Capitalised interest for 2024 is stated two ways.** 159,017 thousand in the debt note (p.F-35,
  $159 million on p.F-29) against 31,714 thousand in the non-cash section of the cash flow statement
  (p.F-11). The filing does not reconcile them.
- **Interest expense has two measures.** Interest expense, net on the statement of operations is
  360,824 thousand for 2024 (p.F-6); total interest expense on debt obligations in the debt note is
  349,203 thousand (p.F-35).
- **The Series C put right date differs inside the S-1.** May 16, 2029 on p.66 against August 15,
  2029 on p.186 and p.211; the final prospectus uses August 15, 2029 throughout.
- **One loan, two names.** The December 2024 $1.0 billion loan is the "Term Loan Facility" in the
  body (p.204) and the "Bridge Loan" in the financial statement notes (p.F-41). The final prospectus
  renames it the 2024 Term Loan Facility.
- **Customers are lettered, not named, in the concentration note.** Customer A's 35% and 62% on
  p.F-13 match the Microsoft percentages given in the text (p.32), but the note itself never says
  Customer A is Microsoft. That match is an inference, not a disclosure.
- **"Data centers" is a defined term** (p.iii): a data center or a portion of one, leased or owned,
  that is receiving active power. The counts are powered sites or portions of sites, not buildings.
  The footprint sentence on p.149 to p.150 words the count of 32 differently from pp.2, 10 and 100.
- **Useful life changed.** The estimate for computing equipment moved from five to six years
  effective January 1, 2023 (p.F-15).
- **Not captured:** the milestone timeline on p.94 and the data-centre maps on p.8 and p.148 are
  images, so any figure shown only there is missing from these notes.
- **Not in the filing at all** (searched for and not found): a price per GPU-hour, a total customer
  count, GPU counts by model, MW by site, a numeric utilisation rate, a weighted-average interest
  rate for total debt, and a capital expenditures KPI (only the cash flow line exists).

## Business model

- **what is sold** [as described in S-1 (FY2024)]: Revenue comes from selling access to AI infrastructure plus proprietary managed software and application services through the CoreWeave Cloud Platform. (p. 95)
- **pricing unit**: Platform access is priced per GPU per hour; storage is sold separately per gigabyte per month. Committed contracts generally carry a fixed dollar price per contracted GPU per hour. (p. 95, 96)
- **pricing level disclosure**: The S-1 states no dollar price per GPU-hour or per gigabyte. The only rate reference found is an unquantified pre-negotiated hourly rate in the MagAI related-party agreement. (p. 188)
- **committed contract share of revenue** [years ended December 31, 2022, 2023, 2024]: Customers buy via committed contracts or on-demand. Committed contracts were 20%, 88% and 96% of revenue in 2022, 2023, 2024; the note's figure includes capacity delivered before commitment start dates. (p. 96, F-25)
- **contract length** [as of December 31, 2024]: Committed contracts typically run two to five years on a take-or-pay basis; weighted-average contract duration was approximately four years as of December 31, 2024. (p. 96, 152)
- **take-or-pay meaning**: Take-or-pay means the customer pays for a minimum committed amount regardless of utilisation; the accounting policy note says payment is required regardless of the level of utilization. (p. v, F-19)
- **contract start and early delivery**: Committed contracts have a predetermined term starting on a fixed date or on delivery of capacity; capacity delivered early is typically billed per hour at the contracted price. (p. 96)
- **customer prepayments - size** [as of December 31, 2022, 2023, and 2024]: A prepayment is typically received at contract signing; the weighted-average prepayment across active contracts was 15% to 25% of TCV at each of the three year-ends. (p. 96)
- **customer prepayments - mechanics**: Prepayments are booked as deferred revenue and generally credited against billings in the final months of the contract; in certain instances they are applied to the first months after go-live. (p. 96)
- **revenue recognition - committed contracts** [accounting policy, FY2022-FY2024]: Committed contracts move through signing, infrastructure purchase and installation, then go-live; revenue is recognised ratably over the contract period from go-live, billed monthly in arrears. (p. 96, F-19)
- **on-demand terms** [accounting policy, FY2022-FY2024]: On-demand access is pay-as-you-go, billed monthly in arrears for hourly usage, with revenue recognised at time of usage; on-demand prepayments are deferred and typically consumed within a few months. (p. 97, F-20)
- **on-demand customers and commitment**: Most on-demand revenue has come from committed-contract customers needing burst capacity; on-demand terms of service require no contractual period and allow termination or reduced usage on limited notice. (p. 45, 97)
- **deferred revenue balance** [as of December 31, 2023 and 2024]: Deferred revenue (current plus non-current) was $2.0 billion and $4.1 billion at December 31, 2023 and 2024, driven by committed-contract prepayments; $0 million, $4 million, $225 million of opening balances was recognised in 2022, 2023, 2024. (p. F-25)
- **deferred revenue - balance sheet split** [as of December 31, 2023 and 2024]: Balance sheet (in thousands): deferred revenue, current $249,831 (2023) and $768,927 (2024); deferred revenue, non-current $1,754,873 (2023) and $3,294,977 (2024). (p. F-4)
- **remaining performance obligations - amount** [as of December 31, 2023 and 2024]: RPO was $15.1 billion at December 31, 2024, up 53% from $9.9 billion at December 31, 2023; the company expects RPO growth in steps, possibly including declines. (p. 93, 101)
- **remaining performance obligations - timing** [as of December 31, 2023 and 2024]: Of $15.1 billion RPO at end-2024, 54% is expected within the first 24 months, 42% in months 25-48, remainder in months 49-72. For $9.9 billion at end-2023: 41%, 40%, remainder. (p. F-25)
- **remaining performance obligations - definition**: RPO is the committed-contract transaction price not yet delivered, net of estimated variable consideration (availability credits, delivery delays), and includes both billed and unbilled amounts. (p. F-20)
- **refund rights and service credits**: Contracts generally give no refund right except for uncured material breach, bankruptcy or insolvency. Most agreements carry service level commitments; service credits and refunds have been provided, including once for insufficient power. (p. 42, 43, F-20)
- **customer expansion** [as of December 31, 2024]: Three of the top five committed customers by TCV added capacity within 12 months: about $7.8 billion added committed spend, roughly 4x initial value. Over 95% of the 2024 revenue increase came from existing customers. (p. 98, 105)
- **cash payback period** [as of December 31, 2024]: Based on committed contracts in effect at December 31, 2024, the company anticipates an average cash payback period of approximately 2.5 years, including customer prepayments, measured per GPU through adjusted EBITDA. (p. 97)
- **capacity after contract expiry**: After a contract ends the company seeks to re-contract the infrastructure or move it to an on-demand pool; it cites redeploying former-generation A100 GPUs into new contracts. (p. 12, 97)
- **customer concentration - top customers** [years ended December 31, 2022, 2023, 2024]: Top three customers were approximately 41% (2022) and 73% (2023) of revenue; top two customers were approximately 77% in 2024, with no other customer at 10% or more in 2024. (p. 12)
- **customer concentration - largest customer** [years ended December 31, 2022, 2023, 2024]: The largest customer was 16% of revenue in 2022 (unnamed); Microsoft was the largest in 2023 and 2024 at 35% and 62% of revenue. (p. 32)
- **customer concentration - 10% customers table** [years ended December 31, 2022, 2023, 2024]: Customers at 10% or more of revenue (lettered, not named): 2022 - D 16%, E 13%, F 12%; 2023 - A 35%, B 21%, C 17%; 2024 - A 62%, C 15%. (p. F-13)
- **accounts receivable concentration** [as of December 31, 2023 and 2024]: Customers A, C and G were 15%, 13% and 56% of net accounts receivable at December 31, 2023; Customer A was 66% at December 31, 2024. (p. F-13)
- **Microsoft agreement** [years ended December 31, 2023 and 2024]: A February 2023 Master Services Agreement covers Microsoft reserved capacity orders; revenue under it was $81 million (2023) and $1.2 billion (2024). Either party may terminate for cause on 45 days' notice of breach. (p. 152)
- **NVIDIA as customer and shareholder** [cumulative to December 31, 2024]: NVIDIA, a holder of more than 5% of capital stock, is also a customer under an April 2023 Master Services Agreement and had paid approximately $320 million in aggregate as of December 31, 2024. (p. 187)
- **MagAI capacity agreement (related party)** [as of December 31, 2024]: August 2024 agreement with a Magnetar fund: approximately $230 million refundable deposit drawn down by usage, four-year initial term plus two-year extension option; no services provided by December 31, 2024. (p. 188)
- **customer segments and named customers**: Customers are AI Natives (AI labs, AI Ops/ML Ops) and AI Enterprises, sold via a direct named-account strategy. Named: Cohere, Mistral AI, Replicate, Meta, IBM, Microsoft, Jane Street, NVIDIA. (p. 93, 98, 151)
- **customer credit quality**: The vast majority of committed-contract revenue is from customers with investment grade ratings; no percentage is given. (p. 96)
- **customers that are also competitors**: Key general-purpose cloud competitors are Amazon (AWS), Google, IBM, Microsoft (Azure) and Oracle, a number of which are also current customers. (p. 37)
- **revenue by geography** [years ended December 31, 2022, 2023, 2024]: Revenue by customer address (in thousands): United States $10,367, $200,469, $1,797,268; all other countries $5,463, $28,474, $118,158 for 2022, 2023, 2024. (p. F-56)
- **employees** [as of December 31, 2024]: 881 employees as of December 31, 2024: 791 in the United States and 90 in five other countries. (p. 151)
- **capacity units the company reports** [as of December 31, 2024]: Capacity is reported as data centers (sites receiving active power, leased or owned), GPUs, MW active power and GW contracted power: 32 data centers, more than 250,000 GPUs, more than 360 MW, approximately 1.3 GW. (p. iii, 2)
- **capacity history** [as of December 31, 2022 and 2023]: End-2022: three data centers, more than 17,000 GPUs, approximately 10 MW active power. End-2023: 10 data centers, more than 53,000 GPUs, more than 70 MW active power. (p. 100)
- **GPU fleet mix** [as of December 31, 2024]: Of more than 250,000 deployed GPUs at December 31, 2024, a majority were NVIDIA Hopper models; no count by GPU model is given. (p. 97)
- **data centers - leased vs owned**: The majority of the data center footprint is leased; the company pays rent plus all or a proportional share of operating expenses including power. It may own data centers over time. (p. 98, 99, 147)
- **data center lease terms** [as of December 31, 2024]: Data center leases generally have initial terms of three to fifteen years, some with extension options of up to ten years; many leases have multi-year terms and fixed capacity. (p. 31, F-31)
- **lease cost and liabilities** [years ended / as of December 31, 2022, 2023, 2024]: In thousands, 2022/2023/2024: operating lease cost $236, $41,515, $288,628; total lease cost $3,413, $61,461, $370,535. Operating lease liabilities $472,442 (2023), $2,602,016 (2024). Remaining operating lease term 6, 8, 9 years. (p. F-31, F-32)
- **leases signed but not yet commenced** [as of December 31, 2024]: Executed leases not yet commenced carry $15.0 billion of estimated undiscounted payments, commencing 2025-2026 with terms of five to sixteen years; further leases could add up to $1.1 billion over seventeen years. (p. F-33)
- **letters of credit backing leases** [as of December 31, 2023 and 2024]: Outstanding letters of credit guaranteeing lease obligations were $174 million and $533 million at December 31, 2023 and 2024; none drawn. (p. F-33)
- **lease events after year-end** [subsequent events, January-March 2025]: February-March 2025: new leases add about $3.4 billion rent over 15 years; a modification with one landlord adds 70 MW contracted power, refunded $304 million escrow, and adds about $1.7 billion rent over 13 years. (p. F-58)
- **power - contracted capacity** [as of December 31, 2024]: An agreement with Core Scientific covers more than 500 MW of capacity as of December 31, 2024; total contracted power was approximately 1.3 GW and active power more than 360 MW at that date. (p. 10)
- **power - procurement and hedging** [FY2023-FY2024]: Power is supplied via third parties at leased or licensed sites. The company entered power purchase agreements in 2023 and 2024, accounted for as derivatives, and says its team hedges power costs. (p. 29, 98, F-19)
- **geographic footprint** [as of December 31, 2024]: Data centers are leased or licensed in 15 U.S. states plus the United Kingdom, Spain and Sweden; European sites had more than 40 MW active power at end-2024; 90% of long-lived assets were in the U.S. (p. 140, 149, 159, F-56)
- **European expansion investment** [announced June 2024]: In June 2024 the company announced a $2.2 billion investment to expand in Spain and Sweden and launch in Norway, expected to more than double its MW of capacity by end of 2025. (p. 150)
- **just-in-time procurement**: Purchase orders are generally placed at contract signing, matched to a committed contract; suppliers are paid on delivery and installation typically takes about three months. (p. 96, 99)
- **GPU financing - DDTL structure**: GPU infrastructure is funded largely by amortizing delayed draw term loans, collateralized by assets underlying contributed customer contracts and pledged contract cash flows, drawn as infrastructure is built. No off-balance-sheet arrangements are reported. (p. 99, 102, 120)
- **financing commitments raised** [as of December 31, 2024]: DDTL commitments total $9.9 billion ($2.3 billion DDTL 1.0, $7.6 billion DDTL 2.0); other facilities: $650 million revolver, $1.0 billion term loan, $1.3 billion OEM financing. Total debt commitments $12.9 billion through end-2024. (p. 2, 10, 99)
- **DDTL 1.0 - borrower entity and security** [as of December 31, 2024]: $2.3 billion facility; borrower CoreWeave Compute Acquisition Co. II, LLC, a direct wholly-owned subsidiary; parent-guaranteed; secured by substantially all CCAC II assets and a 100% equity pledge. Fully drawn at end-2024; matures March 28, 2028. (p. 200, 201)
- **DDTL 2.0 - borrower entity and security** [as of December 31, 2024]: $7.6 billion facility; borrower CoreWeave Compute Acquisition Co. IV, LLC; parent-guaranteed; secured by substantially all CCAC IV assets and a 100% equity pledge. $3.8 billion drawn and $3.8 billion available at end-2024; loans mature five years after funding. (p. 201, 202, 203)
- **DDTL 2.0 - pricing tied to customer credit** [as of December 31, 2024]: DDTL 2.0 spreads follow the financed customer's credit: Term SOFR plus 6.00%, 6.50%, 13.00% and base rate plus 5.00%, 5.50%, 12.00% for specified investment-grade, investment-grade, non-investment-grade contracts. Average rate charged 10.53% at end-2024. (p. 118, 119)
- **DDTL 2.0 - customer-mix covenants**: From January 2026 CCAC IV must keep contract coverage ratios of 1.4x or more; non-investment-grade collateral ratio and non-investment-grade revenue ratio must each be 0.35x or less. (p. 203)
- **OEM equipment financing** [as of December 31, 2024]: Between February and December 2024 an OEM financed equipment with $1.3 billion aggregate notional balance, secured by the financed equipment, terms of two to three years, effective interest rates of 9% to 11%. (p. 205)
- **supplier concentration - NVIDIA GPUs**: All GPUs in the infrastructure are NVIDIA GPUs, which current customers have contractually specified; there are no long-term supply contracts guaranteeing capacity or payment terms. (p. 27, 28)
- **supplier concentration - share of purchases** [years ended December 31, 2022, 2023, 2024]: Three suppliers (unnamed) were 23%, 16%, 10% of total purchases in 2022; 57%, 22%, 11% in 2023; 46%, 16%, 14% in 2024. (p. F-13)
- **OEM partners and upstream supply**: Named OEM partners are Dell and Super Micro, with capacity deployable in as little as two weeks from receipt. NVIDIA depends on TSMC; recent delays with NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs are cited. (p. 9, 27, 29)

### Definitions (condensed; exact wording on the cited page)

- **Adjusted EBITDA:** Non-GAAP: net loss excluding loss on fair value adjustments, D&A, interest expense net, stock-based compensation, other income net, and provision for (benefit from) income taxes. Reconciling items: loss on fair value adjustments; depreciation and amortization; interest expense, net; stock-based compensation; other income, net; provision for (benefit from) income taxes. (p. 111)
- **Adjusted EBITDA margin:** Non-GAAP: adjusted EBITDA divided by revenue. (p. 111)
- **Adjusted operating income (loss):** Non-GAAP: operating income (loss) excluding stock-based compensation. Reconciling items: stock-based compensation. (p. 112)
- **Adjusted operating income (loss) margin:** Non-GAAP: adjusted operating income (loss) divided by revenue. (p. 112)
- **Adjusted net income (loss):** Non-GAAP: net income (loss) attributable to common stockholders excluding loss on fair value adjustments, stock-based compensation, other non-cash or non-routine adjustments, and income tax effects. Reconciling items: loss on fair value adjustments; stock-based compensation; other adjustments (primarily loss on extinguishment of debt); income tax effect adjustments (none material). (p. 112)
- **Adjusted net income (loss) margin:** Non-GAAP: adjusted net income (loss) divided by revenue. (p. 112)
- **Other adjustments (adjusted net income reconciling item):** Reconciling item in adjusted net income (loss): primarily loss on extinguishment of debt from converting the 2021 convertible notes and settling the 2022 senior secured notes. (p. 112)
- **Depreciation and amortization (total):** Total depreciation and amortization, a non-cash expense; shown in the cash flow statement and added back in adjusted EBITDA. (p. 111)
- **Stock-based compensation expense:** Expense for stock-based awards measured at grant-date fair value; added back in all three non-GAAP measures. (p. F-21)
- **Contractual interest expense on debt obligations:** Contractual interest on debt obligations per the debt note, shown before amortization of discounts and issuance costs, PIK interest and the deduction for capitalized interest. (p. F-35)
- **Total interest expense on debt obligations (net of capitalized interest):** Debt note total: contractual interest plus amortization of discounts and issuance costs plus PIK interest, less capitalized interest. (p. F-35)
- **Capitalized interest:** Interest cost capitalized into property and equipment during construction of data centers and purchases of related technology equipment; depreciated with the underlying assets. (p. F-16)
- **Interest expense sensitivity to a 100-basis-point rate change:** Filing's stated sensitivity of interest expense on its floating-rate facilities to a 100-basis-point change in rates. (p. 125)
- **Operating lease cost:** Operating lease cost per the leases note (data centers, offices, equipment); recognized straight-line within operating expenses. Excludes variable lease cost. (p. F-18)
- **Total lease cost:** Sum of operating lease cost, finance lease cost (amortization and interest) and variable lease cost per the leases note. (p. F-31)
- **Availability under existing facilities:** Filing's liquidity table: cash and cash equivalents plus short-term investments plus availability under the revolving credit facility and delayed draw term loan commitments. (p. 117)
- **Total liquidity:** Filing's liquidity table: cash and cash equivalents plus short-term investments plus availability under the revolving credit facility and delayed draw term loan commitments. (p. 117)
- **Remaining performance obligations (RPO):** Aggregate transaction price, net of estimated variable consideration, allocated to undelivered or partially undelivered performance obligations; includes billed and unbilled amounts from committed contracts. (p. F-20)
- **RPO growth, year over year:** Percentage increase in RPO between the two balance sheet dates, as stated by the filing. (p. F-20)
- **RPO expected recognition timing:** Expected timing of revenue recognition of RPO, in 24-month bands from the balance sheet date. (p. F-20)
- **Committed contracts as a percentage of revenue:** Share of revenue from committed contracts: customers reserve capacity on a take-or-pay basis, typically two to five years; includes revenue from capacity delivered before commitment start dates. (p. 96)
- **Weighted-average prepayment as a percentage of TCV:** Customer prepayment typically received at contract signing, recorded as deferred revenue and generally credited against billings in the contract's final months; expressed as a share of total contract value. (p. 96)
- **Expansion commitments from top customers within 12 months of initial purchase:** Cumulative additional committed spend signed by three of the top five committed-contract customers (by TCV) within 12 months of their initial purchase. (p. 98)
- **Expansion multiple on initial contract value:** Cumulative additional committed spend signed by three of the top five committed-contract customers (by TCV) within 12 months of their initial purchase. (p. 98)
- **Average cash payback period:** Anticipated time to break even on investment in GPUs and other property and equipment through adjusted EBITDA; computed per GPU, net of customer prepayments. (p. 97)
- **Largest customer as a percentage of revenue:** Revenue share of the single largest customer; the filing names Microsoft as the largest customer for 2023 and 2024. (p. 32)
- **Top three customers as a percentage of revenue:** Aggregate revenue share of the top customers; the filing reports top three for 2022 and 2023 but top two for 2024. (p. 12)

## Open questions

What the filing does not answer, to be resolved from later 10-Q/10-K filings or earnings calls
before the corresponding driver is treated as more than a placeholder.

- What dollar price per GPU-hour (by GPU model) and per gigabyte-month does CoreWeave charge? The S-1 gives the pricing unit but no price level.
- How many customers does CoreWeave have in total, and how many are under committed contracts? The S-1 states no customer count.
- Who are Customers B, C, D, E, F and G in the significant-customers note, and who is the second of the top two customers in 2024 (Customer C at 15%)? Only Microsoft is identified, in the risk factors.
- Which three suppliers account for 46%, 16% and 14% of 2024 purchases, and what share of purchases is NVIDIA directly versus OEMs such as Dell and Super Micro?
- What share of committed-contract revenue or RPO is from investment-grade customers? The S-1 says only 'the vast majority'.
- How are the 250,000+ GPUs split by model (A100, H100, H200, GB200) and how many GPUs correspond to each MW of active power? The S-1 gives only 'a majority were NVIDIA Hopper models'.
- What are the terms (rate per MW, duration, pass-through of power cost) of the Core Scientific agreement for more than 500 MW? The S-1 gives only the capacity figure.
- What advance rate (percentage of depreciated purchase price) applies to DDTL 2.0 draws for each customer credit tier? The S-1 says the percentage depends on customer credit rating without stating it.
- How much of the approximately 1.3 GW of contracted power is scheduled to become active in each year? The S-1 says only that it will roll out over the coming years.
- What portion of total TCV or RPO is attributable to Microsoft? The S-1 gives Microsoft's share of revenue but not of backlog.
- Is the Core Scientific capacity of more than 500 MW included in the approximately 1.3 GW of total contracted power? The S-1 presents both figures without stating the relationship.
- The filing does not define capital expenditures as a KPI or give a capex total; it discloses only the cash flow line for purchases of property and equipment and a separate non-cash line for unpaid additions.
- The filing says management tracks property and equipment net of construction in progress, but it does not state that figure.
- Adjusted net income (loss) is defined as starting from net loss attributable to common stockholders, yet the FY2024 reconciliation starts from net loss of $(863,448) thousand rather than $(937,765) thousand; the filing does not explain the difference.
- Capitalized interest for FY2024 is $159,017 thousand in the debt note ($159 million in the property and equipment note) but $31,714 thousand in the non-cash section of the cash flow statement; the filing does not reconcile the two.
- Interest expense, net on the statement of operations ($360,824 thousand in FY2024) differs from total interest expense on debt obligations in the debt note ($349,203 thousand); the filing does not reconcile them.
- Total depreciation and amortization is tabulated only in aggregate; apart from the amount stated for technology and infrastructure, its split across cost of revenue and other expense lines is not given.
- TCV (total contract value) is used to rank customers and to size prepayments, but no TCV amount or formal definition is disclosed.
- The cash payback period (approximately 2.5 years) is defined by formula, but its inputs (cash spent per GPU, prepayment per GPU, average annualized committed contract revenue per GPU) are not disclosed.
- The weighted-average prepayment is given only as a 15% to 25% of TCV range covering all three year-ends, not as a point figure for each year.
- No fleet utilization rate, realized price per GPU-hour, revenue per GPU, or revenue per MW is disclosed, and the filing presents no gross profit, gross margin, free cash flow, or backlog measure other than RPO.
- Quarterly figures are given only for the four quarters of 2024 and only for the statement of operations and non-GAAP measures; there is no quarterly cash flow, balance sheet, or RPO data.
- The financial statements letter significant customers A to G without naming them; only Microsoft is named (largest customer in 2023 and 2024), so the identities of the other lettered customers are not given.
- The top-customer aggregate switches basis from top three customers (2022, 2023) to top two customers (2024), so a like-for-like series is not disclosed.
- Pro forma and pro forma as adjusted balance sheet columns and pro forma per-share figures are blank in the S-1 because offering terms were not yet set.
- How much of the approximately 1.3 GW of contracted power becomes active in each year is not stated; the filing only says it expects to roll it out over the coming years.
- Whether the Core Scientific agreement for more than 500 MW is included in the approximately 1.3 GW contracted total, and how much of it was active at December 31, 2024, is not stated.
- The GPU count is not split by model (A100, H100, H200, GH200, GB200); only 'a majority were NVIDIA Hopper models' is disclosed.
- Technology equipment and construction in progress are not split between GPUs/servers, networking and storage, so a cost per GPU cannot be read directly from the filing.
- The filing does not reconcile capitalized interest of $159 million for 2024 in Note 5 with the $31,714 thousand capitalized interest shown in the non-cash section of the cash flow statement.
- Page 149 describes 32 data center facilities as being in the United States across 15 states, while other pages give 32 as the total data center count including Europe; the filing does not reconcile the two.
- The statement that the $2.2 billion European investment will 'more than double our MW of capacity by the end of 2025' does not say whether it refers to European or total capacity, or over what period the $2.2 billion is spent.
- No dollar amount of purchase commitments or open purchase orders for GPUs or other equipment is disclosed; Note 9 contains only letters of credit, the 401(k) plan, indemnifications and litigation.
- Power purchase agreements are disclosed only at fair value; their contracted MW, volume, price and term are not given.
- MW associated with the $15.0 billion of not-yet-commenced leases, the $1.1 billion conditional leases and the $3.4 billion of February-March 2025 leases is not disclosed.
- Lease cost and lease liabilities are not split between data centers, offices and equipment.
- No dates are given for the H100, H200, GH200 or GB200 NVL72 deployment 'firsts', and the 'as little as two weeks' deployment claim is not reconciled with the 'typically three months' installation period.
- No total storage capacity, network size (ports, fiber miles) or largest single-cluster GPU count is disclosed for CoreWeave's own fleet; the 32,000-GPU cabling example is illustrative.
- The basis for the 'up to approximately 20%' MFU improvement (relative or percentage points, which benchmark, which hardware) is not described beyond 'internal testing'.
- What dollar amount of IPO net proceeds will be applied to repay the Term Loan Facility? The S-1 leaves the amount blank on pages 16, 85 and 87.
- Which fixed 2029 date governs the Series C Put Right: May 16, 2029 (risk factor, page 66) or August 15, 2029 (pages 186 and 211)? The S-1 gives both.
- How many Put Shares exist and what is the aggregate cash payment if the Put Right is fully exercised? The S-1 leaves the share count and dollar amounts blank on pages 66, 186 and 211.
- What are the fixed quarterly amortization amounts and the advance-rate percentages by customer credit rating under DDTL 1.0 and DDTL 2.0? The S-1 describes the tests but gives no figures.
- How is the $3.8 billion drawn under DDTL 2.0 split between the specified investment-grade, investment-grade and non-investment-grade pricing tiers? Only the blended actual rate of 10.53% is stated.
