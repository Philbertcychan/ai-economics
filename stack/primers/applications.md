## What this stage is

The products that turn tokens into revenue, and therefore the demand that has to justify every megawatt, wafer and GPU below. Applications differ by an order of magnitude or more in how many tokens they consume per user per day, so this page splits them by consumption rather than by industry.

## How it works

At one end, autonomous coding and research agents run for hours and burn millions of tokens per user per day; a single seat can consume more than a thousand consumer chat users. At the other, consumer assistants have the largest user counts and the lowest tokens per user, and their free tiers set the floor on what a token can be sold for. Enterprise copilots sit between: shallow per user, wide by seat count. Video and image generation are compute-heavy per output and price-sensitive. On-device models move demand away from the data centre altogether.

The question for the chain is not how many users, but how many tokens, and at what price per token those tokens are paid for. Revenue per token, times tokens, has to cover the GPU-hours, which have to cover the capital below them.

## What to watch

- Users, seats and paying share for the largest products.
- Tokens per user per day by tier, where any platform discloses it.
- Subscription and seat prices, and usage-based pricing for agents.
- Where the token growth is coming from: agents versus chat.
- Signs of demand outrunning supply: rate limits, waitlists, price rises.

## How it connects

This is the top of the chain. Tokens demanded here, divided by tokens per GPU-hour, is the GPU-hours the world needs; that number, divided by hours in a year and multiplied by the power per GPU, is the megawatts the bottom of the chain has to deliver.
