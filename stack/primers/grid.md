## What this stage is

A generator and a data centre are only useful once they are connected. This stage is the wires and the equipment between them: transmission lines, substations, transformers and switchgear, and the process by which a utility or grid operator agrees to connect a large new load.

## How it works

A new generator or a new large load applies to the regional grid operator for interconnection and joins a queue. Studies decide what upgrades the connection needs and who pays. In most US regions the queue runs to years, and the study process, not construction, takes most of that time. Large transformers are the physical bottleneck inside the process: few makers, long lead times, and a global order book that data centres now compete for with utilities replacing old equipment.

Utilities plan in multi-year cycles and recover costs from ratepayers, so a sudden gigawatt of demand forces a choice between building ahead of it, charging the data centre for the upgrades, or refusing it. Each region has answered differently.

## What to watch

- Queue size and typical years to connect, by grid operator.
- Large-load interconnection rules: who pays for upgrades, and minimum-take contracts.
- Transformer and switchgear lead times.
- Utility load forecasts and the data-centre share within them.
- Transmission projects approved, and their in-service dates.

## How it connects

The grid turns generation into delivered megawatts. A campus that cannot get a connection either waits, builds its own generation, or moves. The queue is the clock the whole build-out runs on.
