## What this stage is

The accelerator assembled into something that can be plugged in: the GPU, its server, the rack that holds many servers, and the network that ties racks into one cluster. Its unit is the GPU, the thing every plan and every press release counts.

## How it works

A GPU's price is set by its maker; a server's price adds the host processors, memory, storage and assembly; a rack adds power delivery, cooling and the switches inside it; and the cluster adds the optical links and switches between racks, which for the largest systems cost as much as the GPUs they connect. Rack power decides how many racks a megawatt supports. Generations turn over every year or two, and each brings more performance per watt, which is why the useful life of a GPU is argued over.

A handful of server makers assemble most of the world's AI systems on tight schedules; their lead times, and the accelerator maker's own delivery commentary, are the shipment signal.

## What to watch

- Price per GPU, per server and per rack by generation.
- GPUs per rack and rack power.
- Networking cost per GPU and the optics behind it.
- Accelerator revenue and the unit shipments it implies.
- Delivery lead times; the share taken by custom silicon and second suppliers.

## How it connects

A GPU installed and powered is a GPU-hour waiting to be sold. Its price, life and power draw are the inputs to the unit economics of the next stage.
