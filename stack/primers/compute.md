## What this stage is

Accelerator time, sold by the hour. Neoclouds rent GPU-hours under multi-year contracts; hyperscalers rent them and also use them for their own models. This is the stage where the physical chain becomes a dollar chain, and where the company models in this repository live.

## How it works

The price of a GPU-hour has two markets. On-demand list prices are public and fall quickly as a generation ages. Contract prices are private, longer, and stickier, and most neocloud revenue is contracted before the GPUs are switched on. Utilisation, the share of installed hours that earn, is the number operators rarely disclose and the one that decides margins. Customers prepay part of a contract, and the GPUs are financed with loans secured on the contract itself.

The unit economics are simple to state: the hour must earn more than its energy, its facility, its running costs and the repayment of the GPU with interest, over the GPU's useful life.

## What to watch

- On-demand prices by generation and provider, and their rate of decline.
- Contract terms: length, prepayment, take-or-pay.
- Capital spending guidance from the hyperscalers and what it implies in GPUs.
- Utilisation where disclosed; backlog and its conversion to revenue.
- The cost of debt secured on GPU contracts.

## How it connects

A GPU-hour bought by a lab becomes tokens. Its price, against tokens per hour and price per token, decides how the dollar splits between the owner of the GPU and the owner of the model.
