## What this stage is

The building that turns a grid connection into rack-ready megawatts: land, permits, a shell, power distribution, cooling and water, and the fibre to reach the outside world. Its unit is megawatts of IT load, the power available to the computing equipment itself.

## How it works

A data centre is priced by the megawatt it can deliver to racks, and built in months once power is secured. Two ratios matter. Power usage effectiveness is total facility power divided by IT power; the difference is cooling and distribution loss. Rack density is the power one rack draws; GPU racks draw many times what conventional servers do, which is why liquid cooling has become standard for new AI capacity and why older buildings cannot simply be refilled.

Operators either build for themselves or lease from developers under long contracts. The developer takes the land and construction risk; the tenant takes the demand risk. Speed to power is the competitive variable, and it is set by the two stages below this one, not by construction.

## What to watch

- Build cost per megawatt, and how much of it is power and cooling.
- Months from ground-breaking to first power.
- Vacancy and pre-leasing in the main US markets.
- Rack density and the share of new capacity that is liquid-cooled.
- The largest announced campuses: sponsor, megawatts, power source, status.

## How it connects

Megawatts of IT load, divided by the power one GPU and its share of the server draw, give the number of GPUs a site can hold. That conversion is the bridge from the physical world to the unit everyone counts.
