## What this stage is

Three kinds of memory sit around every accelerator. High-bandwidth memory is stacked on the GPU package itself and sets how fast the chip can be fed. Server DRAM holds the working data of the host. NAND flash, in enterprise solid-state drives, stores the datasets and model files. Three companies make almost all HBM, and the same companies make most DRAM and much of the NAND.

## How it works

Memory is a commodity with a capacity cycle. Makers add wafer capacity in large steps, prices fall when supply runs ahead, capital spending is cut, and prices rise when demand catches up. Two things changed that cycle for AI. First, HBM takes several times more wafer area per bit than ordinary DRAM, so moving capacity to HBM removes supply from the rest of the market. Second, AI servers carry far more storage per GPU than conventional servers, and inference at scale needs fast storage for its working memory.

Contract prices, published monthly by the trackers, are the earliest public sign of a shortage. Capital spending guidance is the earliest sign of the next glut.

## What to watch

- HBM stacks and gigabytes per GPU by generation, and HBM supply by maker.
- The share of DRAM wafer capacity going to HBM.
- DRAM and NAND contract price moves, month by month.
- Capital spending guidance and announced fab additions by maker.
- Enterprise SSD demand attributed to AI.

## How it connects

GPUs shipped, times stacks per GPU, is HBM demand. Servers shipped, times storage per server, is NAND demand. Set those against announced capacity and the cycle's next turn is visible before it reaches prices. The 2025 NAND move is the case this page exists to explain.
