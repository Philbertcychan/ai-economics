## What this stage is

The leading-edge logic wafers on which accelerators are made, and the advanced packaging that joins a GPU die to its stacks of high-bandwidth memory. One foundry makes every leading AI accelerator, and one packaging technology, CoWoS, has been the tightest link in the chain since 2023.

## How it works

Chips are bought by the wafer. A wafer yields a number of dies that depends on die size and defect rate, and the largest AI dies are close to the maximum size a lithography tool can print. After fabrication, the die and its memory stacks are mounted on a silicon interposer in a packaging step whose capacity, measured in wafers per month, has grown in steps as new lines are added. Capacity is allocated, not sold on a spot market: the largest customers reserve it a year or more ahead.

The foundry's capital spending and its statements about packaging capacity are the clearest public signal of how many accelerators can exist next year.

## What to watch

- Packaging capacity in wafers per month, and its planned additions.
- Leading-edge wafer prices and the share of foundry revenue from AI.
- Accelerator dies per wafer by generation.
- Foundry capital spending and new fab timing, including the US fabs.
- Allocation statements: who has reserved what.

## How it connects

Wafers per month, times dies per wafer, times yield, is the ceiling on GPUs per month. Each packaged GPU also fixes its memory demand: a set number of HBM stacks per die.
